# A1: Constraint Analysis, FreightBridge Cold-Chain Deployment

**Latency.** A failed refrigeration unit warms the cargo by about 1 °C/min, so the 90 s SLA allows roughly 1.5 °C of rise before the operations centre must be alerted. On-device the budget is easily met: a 30 s feature window sliding every 10 s means a fault is visible within at most 40 s, and inference takes milliseconds, leaving about 50 s of margin. Rural Indian 4G round trips are typically 100–500 ms and can reach several seconds under congestion or handover, so one cloud round trip alone would not break 90 s. The real problem is that cloud inference also needs the uplink to be *up* at the moment of the fault, and in hill regions it often is not (see Connectivity). Latency is therefore met by the edge deterministically, while the cloud meets it only probabilistically.

**Bandwidth.** Per truck per day, assuming 4-byte float samples: temperature at 1 Hz gives 86,400 × 4 B = 0.35 MB; vibration at 500 Hz × 3 axes gives 129.6 M samples × 4 B = 518.4 MB; door events are negligible. The raw total is about **518.7 MB/day**, costing 518.7 × ₹0.10 = **₹51.9 per truck per day**, or ₹4,409/day (about ₹16.1 lakh/year) for 85 trucks, and over ₹50 lakh/year at 265 trucks. With edge inference, only a status heartbeat (about 100 B per minute) and alert messages (about 20 per day at 200 B) are sent: roughly **0.15 MB/day, ₹0.015 per truck**. That is a reduction of about 3,500×, and it keeps the SIM data plan free for the alerts that matter.

**Connectivity.** The Nashik–Aurangabad route loses signal for 35–90 minutes at seven documented locations. A cloud-only system is blind during each gap: with a fault that warms the cargo 1 °C/min, a failure at the start of a 90-minute gap leaves the load to warm for up to 90 minutes with nobody told until coverage returns, far past any vaccine's tolerance. This is exactly the ₹28 lakh Nashik–Aurangabad spoilage pattern. In LogiEdge the edge node classifies locally, writes every inference and alert to a local append-only log, raises an in-cab alert, and a store-and-forward uplink flushes the log to the operations centre when coverage returns, preserving a complete timestamped chain-of-custody record.

**Privacy.** Pharmaceutical clients need proof that cargo-condition data cannot be reached by third parties. With on-device inference, raw sensor streams never leave the truck; only a minimal classification result and alert leave, over an encrypted (TLS) channel to FreightBridge's own backend. This shrinks the attack surface from "every raw stream crossing a public network and a third-party cloud" to "one small authenticated message", and lets FreightBridge state contractually that raw cargo data is processed and retained only on hardware it controls, which also supports audit and data-residency requirements.

**Conclusion.** Latency alone does not rule out the cloud, but bandwidth cost (about 3,500× higher), connectivity gaps of up to 90 minutes, and the privacy commitment together make an edge-first, offline-capable architecture the only acceptable design.
